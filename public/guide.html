Book Studio User Guide

Build an original manuscript with structured planning, chapter generation, independent review and author-controlled approval.

1. Create your book

In your dashboard select New book. Choose Auto Generate to provide an idea in your own words or More Control to edit all design fields yourself. Include a desired title, genre, ending, characters, tone and setting in your description; the book planner should respect details you specify. Review its output before continuing.

2. Choose chapter length

Set the target words per chapter. The system uses a preferred band around your target and checks resulting drafts deterministically. Longer chapters may require additional inference and review passes. Plan restrictions depend on your account tier.

3. Set your writing style

Under the book design, enter style/voice instructions and optionally paste a sample of your own writing. The sample is sent as model context, not used to train a separate model. Samples influence the prose but do not guarantee a perfect voice match.

4. Examine the book plan

Review the overview, canon/world rules, characters, POV assignments and chapter outlines. An outline is a hard boundary: a generated chapter must not borrow events assigned to later chapters.

5. Generate and review

Use Generate + review in the chapter panel. Check the deterministic word count and reviewer notes. If a chapter fails, edit or regenerate it; only approved Canon chapters are considered finalized and used as authoritative history. Sequential generation currently requires chapters to be approved before the next one. Full-book automation may be added as a separate workflow.

6. Export

The Manuscript tab assembles approved Canon chapters, in order, into Markdown. Save or copy the resulting text for reading or publishing.

Accounts, plans and privacy

Book Studio uses ZeroDriveX authentication. Your account tier controls book and chapter limits. Contact the administrator for account setup or tier changes while self-service signup and billing are unavailable. Never paste someone else's unpublished work into your writing sample without permission.
